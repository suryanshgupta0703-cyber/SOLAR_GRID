1. INTERIM MILESTONE OVERVIEW & REVIEW OBJECTIVES
================================================================================
This interim milestone document and accompanying software package (`second/`)
represent the completed technical deliverables for the mid-term evaluation of
"SolarGrid: AI-Based Solar Energy Prediction & Smart Consumption Optimization."

Under residential rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) regimes, intermittent weather
and uncoordinated power draw generate massive grid inefficiencies and financial
penalties. The primary goal of this interim milestone is to mathematically model,
implement, train, and validate the foundational data engineering and artificial
intelligence prediction engines (Phases 1 through 4) that power the entire
optimization pipeline.

Key Review Objectives Accomplished:
1. Developed an automated meteorological and residential consumption data
   pipeline with SQLite persistence (720 hourly records / 30 full days).
2. Built a hybrid machine learning architecture combining Deep Learning
   (PyTorch 2-layer LSTM) and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost).
3. Achieved rigorous validation on holdout test data:
   - Solar Generation:  MAE = 1.34 kW, RMSE = 3.13 kW, MAPE = 9.14%
   - Community Demand:  MAE = 0.91 kW, RMSE = 2.21 kW, MAPE = 6.41%
4. Implemented interactive 24-hour predictive curves identifying solar surplus
   windows (10:00 AM – 3:00 PM), mathematically establishing the decision boundary
   for the upcoming Heuristic Load Scheduler.
5. Engineered a self-contained demonstration application (`app_interim.py`) allowing
   the evaluation committee to inspect raw data, test models, and run live inference.

3. PHASE 1 & 2: DATA ENGINEERING & METEOROLOGICAL PIPELINE
================================================================================
3.1 Synthetic Physics Engine & Weather Variables:
Due to the unavailability of proprietary IoT sensor feeds at the development
stage, a realistic meteorological and physics generator (`data_gen.py`) was
constructed using empirical solar geometry equations:

• Solar Elevation & Clear-Sky Irradiance ($I_{cs}$):
  Modeled using a diurnal sinusoidal arc bounded between sunrise (06:00) and
  sunset (18:00), peaking at solar noon (12:00–13:00) with a zenith flux of 1000 W/m²:
  $$I_{cs}(t) = \max\left(0, 1000 \cdot \sin\left(\frac{\pi (t - 6)}{12}\right)\right)$$

• Cloud Cover Attenuation:
  Cloud cover percentage $C \in [0, 100]$ acts as an optical attenuation filter:
  $$I_{actual}(t) = I_{cs}(t) \cdot \left(1 - 0.75 \cdot \left(\frac{C}{100}\right)^2\right) + \epsilon$$

• Photovoltaic Conversion Efficiency:
  Solar power output (kW) accounts for system capacity ($P_{rated} = 25\text{ kW}$),
  panel temperature derating ($-0.4\%/^\circ\text{C}$ above $25^\circ\text{C}$), and inverter loss:
  $$P_{solar}(t) = P_{rated} \cdot \left(\frac{I_{actual}(t)}{1000}\right) \cdot [1 - 0.004 \cdot (T_{ambient} - 25)] \cdot \eta_{inverter}$$

3.2 Dual-Peak Community Demand Modeling:
Residential community load is modeled to reflect realistic human behavior:
- Morning Peak (07:00 – 09:30): Water heating, breakfast prep, community pumps.
- Afternoon Valley (11:00 – 16:00): Low occupancy, base load (refrigeration, standby).
- Evening Peak (18:30 – 22:30): HVAC, lighting, cooking, home entertainment.
- Baseline base load: 6.0 kW; Peak demand: Up to 28.5 kW.

3.3 Relational SQLite Database Architecture:
The system uses an embedded zero-configuration SQLite database (`solargrid.db`)
with full indexing on timestamps:
- Table `historical_readings`: 720 records spanning 30 days.
  Columns: `timestamp`, `solar_generation_kw`, `grid_demand_kw`,
           `solar_irradiance`, `cloud_cover`, `temperature_c`.
- Table `appliances`: Pre-configured load profiles for water pumps & EV chargers.
- Table `system_logs`: Audit trail for model training and data generation runs.

4. PHASE 3 & 4: MACHINE LEARNING FORECASTING ENGINE
================================================================================
The core requirement is to predict 24 hours ahead for both solar generation and
community demand. A hybrid model structure was chosen to capture both sequential
dependencies and feature interaction non-linearities:

4.1 Deep Learning Model: PyTorch Stacked LSTM (`models/lstm_model.py`):
- Architecture:
  * Input layer: 6 features (Irradiance, Cloud Cover, Temp, Hour_sin, Hour_cos, Is_Weekend)
  * Hidden layers: 2 stacked LSTM layers with 64 hidden units each
  * Dropout: 0.15 between LSTM layers to prevent overfitting
  * Fully connected head: Linear(64 -> 32) -> ReLU -> Linear(32 -> 1)
  * Sequence lookback window: 24 preceding hours ($t-24$ to $t-1$) to predict hour $t$
- Optimizer: Adam with learning rate $\alpha = 0.002$, weight decay $10^{-4}$
- Loss function: Mean Squared Error (MSE)

4.2 Extreme Gradient Boosting: XGBoost Regressor (`models/train_models.py`):
- Hyper-parameters:
  * `n_estimators`: 200 trees
  * `learning_rate`: 0.05 (conservative shrinkage)
  * `max_depth`: 5
  * `subsample`: 0.85
  * `colsample_bytree`: 0.85
- Purpose: Robust regression over non-linear weather interaction thresholds
  (e.g., cloud cover cutoffs and temperature inflection points).

4.3 Hybrid Ensemble Strategy:
- Final Solar Forecast:
  $$\hat{Y}_{solar} = 0.60 \cdot \hat{Y}_{XGBoost} + 0.40 \cdot \hat{Y}_{LSTM}$$
  Weights were determined by validation grid search; XGBoost excels on edge weather
  spikes while LSTM preserves diurnal smoothness.
- Demand Forecast:
  Driven by XGBoost with day-of-week, hour encodings, and temperature correlation.

4.4 Cyclical Temporal Encoding:
To prevent artificial discontinuity between hour 23 (23:00) and hour 0 (00:00):
$$\text{Hour}_{sin} = \sin\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot t}{24}\right), \quad \text{Hour}_{cos} = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot t}{24}\right)$$

5. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS, METRICS & BENCHMARK VALIDATION
================================================================================
5.1 Holdout Test Set Evaluation:
The 720-hour dataset was split chronologically:
- Training Set: First 80% (576 hours / 24 days)
- Holdout Evaluation Set: Last 20% (144 hours / 6 days)

Evaluation Metrics:
- Mean Absolute Error (MAE): $\frac{1}{n}\sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$
- Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): $\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$
- Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE): $\frac{100\%}{n}\sum \left|\frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{y_i}\right|$ (computed during daylight hours for solar)

Official Test Results:
┌───────────────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
│ Target Variable           │ MAE (kW)     │ RMSE (kW)    │ MAPE (%)     │
├───────────────────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
│ Solar Generation (kW)     │ 1.34 kW      │ 3.13 kW      │ 9.14%        │
│ Community Demand (kW)     │ 0.91 kW      │ 2.21 kW      │ 6.41%        │
└───────────────────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘

5.2 Model Comparison (Solar Forecasting):
┌───────────────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐

6. ENERGY BALANCE ANALYSIS: THE BRIDGE TO LOAD SCHEDULING
================================================================================
The net energy balance $B(t)$ for each hour $t \in [0, 23]$ is defined as:
$$B(t) = \hat{P}_{solar}(t) - \hat{P}_{demand}(t)$$

Two distinct operational domains emerge:
1. Surplus Window ($B(t) > 0$):
   Typically occurs between 10:00 and 15:00, reaching peak net surplus of +14.2 kW.
   Without intelligent scheduling, this energy is exported back to the grid at
   a negligible feed-in rate (Rs 2.50/kWh).
2. Deficit Window ($B(t) < 0$):
   Occurs during night and morning/evening peak hours, requiring grid draw at
   peak commercial rates (Rs 8.50/kWh).

Conclusion for the Evaluation Committee:
The prediction engine reliably maps the exact shape and magnitude of the surplus
window 24 hours in advance. This output directly feeds the Phase 5 Heuristic
Optimization Engine to schedule flexible loads (EVs and water pumps) into this
surplus window without human intervention.
