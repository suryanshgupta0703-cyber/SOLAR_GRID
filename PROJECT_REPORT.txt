1. PROJECT OVERVIEW & ABSTRACT
================================================================================
SolarGrid is an AI-powered energy management and load-scheduling software
system engineered specifically for modern residential housing societies and
prosumer communities equipped with rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) arrays.

As urbanization expands and residential societies transition from passive
electricity consumers into active "prosumers" (producers and consumers),
societies face severe economic penalties under changing utility tariff structures,
such as Time-of-Day (ToD) peak pricing and reduced feed-in credits (net-metering
rollbacks). Under these regulations, selling surplus power back to the grid yields
far lower returns than self-consuming it on-site.

SolarGrid bridges the critical operational gap between volatile solar power
generation and community electricity demand. By combining deep learning (LSTM)
and gradient boosting (XGBoost) with real-time meteorological variables,
SolarGrid predicts next-day solar electricity generation and hourly consumption
profiles. A heuristic optimization engine dynamically allocates high-draw,
flexible community loads—such as water supply pumps and Electric Vehicle (EV)
chargers—into predicted solar surplus windows (e.g., 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM).

The system maximizes localized solar self-consumption, minimizes peak grid
dependency, slashes electricity bills, prevents grid overload penalties, and
delivers an intuitive, real-time visual dashboard for housing society managers.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION
================================================================================
Rooftop solar installations in residential communities suffer from severe
operational inefficiencies:

1. Weather-Dependent Volatility:
   Solar generation fluctuates dynamically throughout the day due to solar
   irradiance variations, cloud cover attenuation, and ambient temperature shifts.

2. Lack of Visibility & Predictive Tools:
   Society managers rely on guesswork, visual sky observation, or basic inverter
   displays that only show instantaneous wattage without next-day forecasting or
   historical trend analytics.

3. Sub-Optimal Appliance & Load Timing:
   Heavy electrical loads—central community water pumps (3.5 kW) and EV charging
   stations (7.4 kW)—are typically operated during morning (7–9 AM) or evening
   (7–10 PM) peak grid tariff hours when solar output is either negligible or zero.

4. Unrewarded Feed-In & Financial Penalties:
   Uncoordinated energy usage causes solar power generated during peak midday
   hours to be exported to the grid at low feed-in tariffs, while evening peak
   demand is purchased back at high commercial grid rates.

5. Inverter Limitations:
   Standard inverters provide no automated intelligence, historical logging,
   or actionable dispatch recommendations.

3. PROJECT OBJECTIVES & SOFTWARE SCOPE
================================================================================
PRIMARY OBJECTIVES:
• Accurate Solar Yield Forecasting: 24-hour advance forecasting of hourly rooftop
  solar generation using meteorological features (irradiance, cloud cover, temp).
• Community Load Demand Modeling: Accurate hourly community load prediction based
  on historical residential consumption patterns.
• Smart Heuristic Load Scheduling: Automated algorithm matching high-draw
  controllable appliances to periods of solar surplus with priority handling.
• Electricity Cost & Tariff Optimization: Quantifiable reduction in kilowatt-hour
  grid draw and rupee cost savings.
• Intuitive User Interface & Analytics: High-performance dashboard providing
  real-time energy balance curves, net power tracking, and scheduled timelines.

SCOPE DEFINITION (as defined in Technical Overview PDF):
• Software-Only Scope: Fully operable and demonstrable with historical datasets
  and meteorological features. Does not require costly battery energy storage
  systems (BESS) or physical solar hardware.
• Decision-Support Scope: Generates automated scheduling recommendations and
  clear operational time windows. Physical electrical relays/breakers are not
  required for prototype demonstration.
• Consistent Technology Stack: Standardized on Python 3.x, PyTorch LSTM,
  XGBoost, SQLite, Streamlit, and Plotly.

4. V.E.T.S. FRAMEWORK JUSTIFICATION
================================================================================
1. VALUE (Economic & Operational ROI):
   Directly increases self-consumption ratio of solar energy. By consuming solar
   surplus on-site for water pumping and EV charging, the community avoids buying
   expensive grid electricity during peak ToD tariff hours and sidesteps low
   feed-in net metering returns.

2. EFFICIENCY & ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT:
   Software-defined optimization eliminates the high capital cost and chemical
   footprint of dedicated battery banks. Replaces fossil-fuel grid power draw
   with clean, localized solar energy, cutting community carbon emissions.

3. TECHNOLOGY (Engineering Depth & Hybrid ML):
   Leverages complementary architectures:
   - PyTorch LSTM learns sequential temporal patterns and carryover effects.
   - XGBoost captures non-linear meteorological feature interactions.
   - Weighted ensemble provides robust 24-hour ahead predictions.
   - Heuristic scheduling engine prevents appliance conflict and double-counting.

4. SCALABILITY & ARCHITECTURE:
   Clean modular separation (Database, ML Modeling, Scheduling, UI Presentation)
   allows seamless adaptation to any community scale, varying solar panel
   capacities, and real-time weather API feeds.

6. MATHEMATICAL MODELING & ALGORITHMS
================================================================================

6.1 Weather & Physics Synthetic Data Engine (`data_gen.py`):
- Solar Irradiance:
    Daylight Fraction = clip((hour - 6) / 12, 0, 1)
    Base Irradiance   = 1000 * sin(pi * Daylight Fraction)
    Transmittance     = 1.0 - 0.9 * (cloud_cover / 100)
    Irradiance (W/m²) = clip(Base Irradiance * Transmittance, 0, 1100)
- Solar Power Generation:
    P_solar (kW) = Irradiance * Panel Area (300 m²) * Efficiency (18%) * 0.001
- Baseline Community Demand (Dual-Peak Residential Profile):
    Morning Peak: 0.7 * exp(-0.5 * ((hour - 8.0) / 1.5)^2)
    Evening Peak: 1.0 * exp(-0.5 * ((hour - 19.5) / 1.8)^2)
    Demand (kW) = Peak Capacity * (Base Load + Peaks) + Gaussian Noise

6.2 Machine Learning Architecture:
- LSTM Solar Model (`models/lstm_model.py`):
    Architecture: Input(8) -> LSTM(hidden=64, layers=2, dropout=0.2) -> FC(1)
    Loss: Mean Squared Error (MSE), Optimizer: Adam (lr=1e-3, grad_clip=1.0)
- XGBoost Models (`models/train_models.py`):
    Target 1: Solar Generation (kW)
    Target 2: Baseline Electricity Demand (kW)
    Hyperparameters: n_estimators=300, max_depth=6, lr=0.05, tree_method="hist"
- Ensemble Formulation (`models/forecast.py`):
    Solar_forecast(h)  = 0.55 * LSTM_pred(h) + 0.45 * XGB_solar_pred(h)
    Demand_forecast(h) = XGB_demand_pred(h)

6.3 Heuristic Scheduling Algorithm (`scheduler/heuristic.py`):
  Inputs: Solar array S[0..23], Demand array D[0..23], Appliance list A
  1. Compute Net Energy: Net[h] = S[h] - D[h] for h in 0..23
  2. Sort appliances by priority ascending (e.g., Water Pump=1, EV Charger=2)
  3. For each appliance a in sorted(A):
       best_start = a.window_start
       max_surplus = -infinity
       For start_h in a.window_start .. a.window_end:
           end_h = start_h + a.duration_hours
           If end_h > 24: continue
           window_surplus = sum(Net[start_h : end_h])
           If window_surplus > max_surplus:
               max_surplus = window_surplus
               best_start = start_h
       Schedule appliance a to [best_start, best_start + a.duration_hours]
       Deduct appliance load: Net[best_start : end_h] -= a.power_kw
  4. Compute savings, grid draw, and benchmark against conventional baseline.

10. SETUP, INSTALLATION & EXECUTION GUIDE
================================================================================

Follow these exact steps to run the complete SolarGrid system locally:

Step 1: Navigate to the project root directory:
    cd solargrid

Step 2: Install dependencies (using standard pip or uv):
    pip install -r requirements.txt

Step 3: Generate synthetic database records (if starting fresh):
    python data_gen.py

Step 4: Train the Machine Learning models (generates all weights & metrics):
    python models/train_models.py

Step 5: Launch the interactive Streamlit Dashboard:
    streamlit run app.py

Step 6: Open your web browser at:
    http://localhost:8501
